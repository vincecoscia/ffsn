# The Bench Is a Confession, and Sable Ridge Signed It

## The argument, stated early so nobody has to hunt for it

This is opinion, and here it is: week seven of this league was decided by a man reading an injury report at noon and believing it more than he believed his own eyes, and I have watched enough of these Sundays to tell you that is how most seasons actually end — not in a blowout, in a hesitation.

The blowout happened too. Cedar Falls put 141.6 on Ashgrove's 96.4 and the standings tidied themselves up accordingly. Gravel Pit is 7-0. Halyard Bay won a game the projections had already given away. Curtis has the rest of the scores and Nina has the column that holds them; I only need one game and one number, and the number is 5.3.

A season is a mortgage. You pay it in small, boring decisions, and one missed payment is remembered longer than every month you were fine.

## Five points, and a bench nobody wants to look at

Gravel Pit Grinders 134.2, Sable Ridge Sentinels 128.9, margin 5.3, and the box score reads like an accident until you stop at the bench line: Sable Ridge left 78.6 points there against Gravel Pit's 41.3. I'd call that a strategy if I thought anyone had chosen it.

The specific wound is Duke Ellery, benched, who would have replaced Cole Vandermeer in the lineup for a gain of 17.9 points. Vandermeer started and returned 6.2 against a projection of 15.8. Ivan Petrosky ran for 28.6 for the Sentinels and it bought them nothing, because a great performance next to a benched one is just a nicer way to lose.

Ruth Tanaka of the Sable Ridge Sentinels was asked why Ellery sat, and she answered plainly, which I'll grant her:

:::quote{id=Q1}

And then she said the part that made me put the pen down for a second:

:::quote{id=Q2}

Ruth, we've disagreed all year and you know exactly how I've phrased it. So here is the last time I write your name this season: you were afraid of a zero and you paid 17.9 for the insurance. I made a claim back in week five that this team does not win a game before Thanksgiving, and it's still open, and Sunday was the first time all season I'd have preferred to be wrong.

Meanwhile Dana Whitlock of the Gravel Pit Grinders is 7-0 and 41.3 points of his own team never left the couch, which tells you the perfect record and the perfect week are not the same animal.

A missed payment is still a payment somebody else collected.

## The four games that behaved themselves

Cedar Falls Cormorants 141.6, Ashgrove Anvils 96.4, and Cass Lindqvist did 33.4 of it by himself, nearly double a 17.6 projection. On the other side Bo Tremaine was projected 16.2 and delivered 4.8, which is the kind of afternoon that makes a manager stop refreshing. Cedar Falls also spent 12 on Trey Ballinger midweek, which is the least dramatic sentence in this column and possibly the smartest. Bea Coleridge of the Ashgrove Anvils declined to comment. I asked. She's entitled to silence, and I'm entitled to the column.

Quarry Road Quakers 118.7, Milltown Mudlarks 112.3, and the projections had Milltown ahead at 119.8 to 114.5, so this was the week the forecast got read by somebody who never goes outside. Femi Adeyemi gave the Mudlarks 26.3 and lost anyway; Nico Barrantes gave the Quakers 19.7 from tight end against a 10.4 projection, and a tight end doubling his number is how close games quietly get decided. Milltown spent 34 on Rudy Halvorsen on Wednesday morning, which is a man trying, and trying is not nothing. Owen Radcliffe of the Milltown Mudlarks did not respond. He's entitled to that too.

Halyard Bay Harriers 127.5, Ninth Street Nightjars 103.8, and this is the only upset on the board — Ninth Street was projected 126.2 and finished 103.8, a gap of 22.4 between what was promised and what arrived. Gil Provost threw for 29.9. Tobias Reneau was projected 19.3 and came back with 8.1. And Ninth Street had 61.5 on the bench, the second-loudest bench of the week, which is a sentence I did not enjoy typing twice.

Foundry District Foxes 121.4, Pike County Pilots 99.6. Dane Kowalczyk carried 27.2 of the Foxes' afternoon; Ellis Marchetti's 12.5 was the best thing Pike County started, and when your best starter is under his projection the rest of the lineup is a rumor. Pike County left 55.3 on the bench. The record does not tell me who they should have started instead, and I'm not going to invent him.

Four games, four teams that got what their week deserved. That is a rarer week in this league than anybody admits.

## Who showed up, and who mailed it

Cass Lindqvist was the best player in the conference on Sunday: 33.4 for Cedar Falls, against a 17.6 projection, in a game that stopped being a game before dinner. Rex Dolan's 31.8 for Gravel Pit is the reason a 7-0 team stayed 7-0 by 5.3 instead of losing. Gil Provost's 29.9 for Halyard Bay and Ivan Petrosky's 28.6 for Sable Ridge were the same performance in different weather — one of them decided a game, one of them was wasted by a decision made hours earlier.

The duds are simpler and sadder. Bo Tremaine, 4.8 for Ashgrove on a 16.2 projection. Tobias Reneau, 8.1 for Ninth Street on 19.3. Cole Vandermeer, 6.2 for Sable Ridge on 15.8, and he is only in this paragraph because somebody chose him over a man who scored 24.1 in street clothes.

A projection is a promise made by someone who won't be there to keep it.

## On the record, for what it's worth

Two managers said something worth arguing with this week, and one of them was undefeated.

Dana Whitlock of the Gravel Pit Grinders, asked about starting 7-0:

:::quote{id=Q3}

Dana, I've credited your waiver discipline before and I'll credit your restraint now, but I want to argue with you as a peer, because 812.4 points for is not a trophy either and it is also not an accident. You are not lucky. You are just not finished, and those are different sentences.

Lena Okafor of the Halyard Bay Harriers, asked about the upset over Ninth Street:

:::quote{id=Q4}

That is the whole business in eleven words. Nobody in this conference is supposed to win anything; they just line up and find out. Somebody is going to learn something from this week. I've done this long enough not to bet on who.
