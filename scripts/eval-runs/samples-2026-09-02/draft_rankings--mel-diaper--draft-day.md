# Mel Diaper's Ironclad Draft Grades: Three Rounds, Thirty Receipts

## The Board, The Deltas, The Truth

Thirty picks. Ten teams. PPR. That's the whole crime scene and I've got the whole board in front of me.

Here's how this desk grades: projected starter points get 40 percent, what you paid against ADP gets 40 percent, bench depth gets the last 20. That's it. No vibes. A pick taken later than its ADP is value. A pick taken earlier than its ADP is a reach, and I am going to name it, and I am going to print the number next to it so nobody gets to argue with the tone instead of the math.

Two things jumped off the board immediately. First, quarterbacks: Ezra Kowalski projects for 341.2 and Lorenzo Bianchi for 328.6, and both of them were on the clock in the first three rounds. Second, tight end went weird early — Dominic Salazar at seven, Casper Whitfield at sixteen, and two more before pick thirty.

I asked Ruth Tanaka of the Sable Ridge Sentinels about her board. Ruth Tanaka has not gotten back to me. That's a fact and it's the only one I'll offer about it.

Grades below. Bring a helmet.

## Ten Teams, Ten Grades, No Mercy

**Sable Ridge Sentinels (Ruth Tanaka) — B**

Jaylon Petrie at 1.1 with an ADP of 1.2. That's the chalk, and the chalk projects 288.4, the highest running back number on this board. Fine. You cannot miss the first pick when the first pick is that obvious. Then it got loud: Ansel Rutherford at twenty against an ADP of 34.1, a delta of -14.1, the largest reach in the entire draft. A rookie. Fourteen picks of air. Bodhi Ferraro at twenty-one against 29.6 is another -8.6. Tanaka spent two consecutive picks paying a combined 14.1 and 8.6 over market for guys who project 204.6 and 196.4. Petrie carries this roster, and the second and third round tried to sink it. I asked Tanaka about the board. Tanaka has not gotten back to me. Last season I called her first-rounder nineteen picks of air; the receipt this year reads -14.1, so nothing about my method has changed and neither has hers.

**Pike County Pilots (Hollis Vance) — A**

Best draft in the conference and it isn't close. Marquis Deveraux at two, ADP 3.4, a delta of -1.4 — that is essentially free, and 271.6 projected is the top wideout number on the board. Then the position that decides leagues. Vance was asked about taking a quarterback in the second round.

:::quote{id=Q1}

Done streaming, sure — but the reason it works isn't the philosophy, it's that Kowalski projects 341.2, which is 53 points north of Petrie's 288.4, the best projection at any position here. Then Kwame Odenigbo at twenty-two against an ADP of 41.2, a delta of -19.2, the biggest gap on the entire board. Nineteen picks early for a tight end who projects 178.3. That's the one soft spot in an otherwise pristine board, and the elite quarterback number covers it.

**Ashgrove Anvils (Bea Coleridge) — A-**

Trent Okonjo at three with an ADP of 2.8 — delta +0.2, the only positive-delta pick in the entire first round and a 265.2 projection to go with it. That is how you open. Coleridge was asked about the Dashiell Moreau pick at eighteen.

:::quote{id=Q2}

Every draft you ever enter? Then enter one where Moreau's ADP isn't 27.8, because at eighteen that's a delta of -9.8 for 209.7 projected points. Conviction is not value. What saves the block is Finnegan Doyle at twenty-three, ADP 26.4, only -3.4 for 192.8 — reasonable freight in round three. Okonjo plus Doyle is a real backfield.

**Milltown Mudlarks (Owen Radcliffe) — B+**

Silas Brenner at four, ADP 5.1, delta -1.1, 258.9 projected. Clean. Roman Ishikawa at seventeen against 19.4 is -2.4 and 212.5 — also clean. Then Zeke Abernathy at twenty-four against an ADP of 31.7, a delta of -7.7 for 189.5. Radcliffe drafted three straight solid contributors and then paid nearly eight picks of premium for the fourth. No quarterback through three rounds while Kowalski went at nineteen and Bianchi at twenty-five. That's the gap in the grade.

**Foundry District Foxes (Sam Kestrel) — A-**

Emory Vasquez at five, ADP 4.6, delta +0.4, 254.3 projected — a positive delta on a top-five running back. Then Casper Whitfield at sixteen against an ADP of 24.9, a delta of -8.9 for 198.2, which is nearly nine picks paid for a tight end. I don't love the freight and I don't love the position. What I do love is pick twenty-five: Lorenzo Bianchi, ADP 35.9, delta -10.9, projecting 328.6. Yes, that's ten-plus picks early. When the return is the second-best projection on the board, I'll sign for it.

**Halyard Bay Harriers (Lena Okafor) — B**

Kaden Ruthford at six, ADP 7.2, delta -1.2, 249.8 projected. Theo Delacroix at fifteen, ADP 16.3, delta -1.3, 216.8. Two disciplined picks, barely a pick over market on either. Then Otto Lindgren at twenty-six with an ADP of 23.8 — delta +2.2, actual value, but the projection is 186.2, the second-lowest running back number in the top thirty. Okafor bought the discount on a guy the market already discounted. Two receivers and no quarterback through three. Solid, unspectacular, and thin at the position that scores the most.

**Ninth Street Nightjars (Trevor Ashby) — C+**

Here's the one that made me put the coffee down. Dominic Salazar, a tight end, at pick seven overall, ADP 12.4, delta -5.4. Five and a half picks early for a rookie who projects 231.5 — the lowest first-round projection of the ten. Then Malik Ostrowski at fourteen against 18.6, another -4.6 for 219.4. Then Rafael Quintero at twenty-seven against an ADP of 38.4, a delta of -11.4 for 183.7. Three picks, three reaches, totalling 5.4 plus 4.6 plus 11.4 of premium paid. Ashby did not get outbid on anybody — he bid against himself three times. Fine. I had Salazar wrong last year and the tight end scored; that's the one you get.

**Quarry Road Quakers (Priya Nandi) — B-**

Beckett Nakamura at eight with an ADP of 6.9 — delta +1.1, the biggest positive delta in round one, and 244.7 projected. That is a genuinely excellent open. Griffin Lasseter at thirteen against 14.1 is -1.1, market price. Then Soren Baumgartner at twenty-eight against an ADP of 44.6, a delta of -16.6, for a tight end projecting 165.9 — the lowest projection of any player in the first three rounds. Nandi found 1.1 picks of value in round one and then spent 16.6 of it back in round three. The board gave her Baumgartner sixteen picks later and she declined the gift.

**Cedar Falls Cormorants (Marcus Bly) — B+**

Xavier Mbeki at nine, ADP 9.8, delta -0.8, 240.1 projected. Amari Fontenot at twelve against an ADP of 15.2 — delta -3.2, three picks of freight, 225.6 projected. Then the best pure value play of round three: Idris Calloway at twenty-nine with an ADP of 21.3, a delta of +7.7. Bly let the board come to him and got a back the market wanted eight picks earlier. The catch is the projection — 181.4 — so this is a discount on a modest asset, not a windfall. Still the most patient board in the conference. No quarterback, which is the ceiling on the grade.

**Gravel Pit Grinders (Dana Whitlock) — B**

Whitlock drafted from the turn and did the sensible thing with it. Rowan Achebe at ten, ADP 11.5, delta -1.5, 236.4. Nikolai Brandt at eleven, ADP 13.7, delta -2.7, 228.3. Percival Nakagawa at thirty, ADP 33.5, delta -3.5, 178.9. Every single pick a reach, none of them by more than 3.5. That's the profile of a manager who wanted his guy and paid a tip rather than a ransom, and I respect the discipline more than the outcome. Three picks, three positions of the two that matter in PPR, and 643.6 in combined projection from 236.4 plus 228.3 plus 178.9. Adequate. Uninspired. Safe.

## The Steals: Receipts Only

Lorenzo Bianchi at twenty-five is the pick of the draft. ADP 35.9, delta -10.9, and a 328.6 projection — Foundry District paid eleven picks and got the number two scorer on the board. That's an arbitrage, not a reach.

Ezra Kowalski at nineteen is the runner-up. ADP 22.5, delta -3.5, projecting 341.2. Highest projection at any position, and Pike County paid three and a half picks for it.

Beckett Nakamura at eight: delta +1.1, 244.7 projected. The only round-one back Quarry Road could have taken at a discount, and she took him.

And Trent Okonjo at three, delta +0.2, 265.2. Ashgrove got the consensus second-best running back at a positive delta. Boring. Correct.

## The Reaches: Somebody Pay For This

Ansel Rutherford at twenty. ADP 34.1. Delta -14.1. That is the single largest gap on this board and Sable Ridge paid it for a rookie projecting 204.6, which is LOWER than four backs still available by ADP logic. Fourteen picks of air.

Kwame Odenigbo at twenty-two. ADP 41.2. Delta -19.2, the widest gap of the thirty, spent on a tight end projecting 178.3. Pike County's one unforced error.

Soren Baumgartner at twenty-eight. ADP 44.6. Delta -16.6 for 165.9 projected — the lowest number in three rounds paid at the highest premium in three rounds.

And Otto Lindgren at twenty-six: yes, delta +2.2 is a discount, but 186.2 is what the discount bought. Halyard Bay saved change on a coupon nobody else wanted.

## Where This Ends Up

Order off projected starter points and what each board paid to get them.

1. Pike County Pilots — Kowalski's 341.2 plus Deveraux's 271.6 is 612.8 from two players. Nobody else is close.
2. Foundry District Foxes — Bianchi 328.6 plus Vasquez 254.3 is 582.9, and Vasquez came at a positive delta.
3. Ashgrove Anvils — positive-delta Okonjo, plus 209.7 and 192.8 behind him.
4. Sable Ridge Sentinels — Petrie's 288.4 drags a 14.1-pick reach uphill.
5. Milltown Mudlarks.
6. Cedar Falls Cormorants.
7. Gravel Pit Grinders.
8. Quarry Road Quakers.
9. Halyard Bay Harriers.
10. Ninth Street Nightjars — three reaches totalling 5.4, 4.6 and 11.4 of premium, and the lowest first-round projection at 231.5.

I'll say it plainly: Pike County finishes top three, and Ninth Street finishes bottom three.
