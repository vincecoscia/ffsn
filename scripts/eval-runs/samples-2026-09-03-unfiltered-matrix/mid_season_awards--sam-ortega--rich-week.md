# Mid-Season Awards: The Grinders Take the Podium, the Sentinels Take the Microphone

## The Ceremony Is a Folding Table on the Sideline

Week 7 is done. Ten teams, fourteen weeks of regular season, six playoff spots, and nobody has cleared their throat for a speech yet.

So I bring the awards to them.

The Gravel Pit Grinders are 7-0 and first. The Sable Ridge Sentinels are 0-7 and tenth. Everything in between is a scramble, and I have a microphone and a notebook and two managers who did not want to talk this week.

Nina has the rest of the numbers. I have the people.

## MVP: The Quarterback Who Beat His Own Projection by Nine

Rex Dolan of the Gravel Pit Grinders put up 31.8 against a projection of 22.4 on Sunday. The Grinders won by 5.3. You can do that subtraction yourself and see the whole season in it.

I catch Dana Whitlock of the Gravel Pit Grinders after the final. I ask what 7-0 feels like at the midpoint.

:::quote{id=Q3}

I ask the follow-up she would enjoy: what would be a trophy. Dana does not add to it. I write down the first answer and leave it alone, because it was already the whole answer.

The Grinders have 812.4 points for, most in the league. They host the Cedar Falls Cormorants and their 788.9 in Week 8. That is the two highest-scoring teams in the conference in the same room.

## Biggest Disappointment: 8.1 When You Needed 19.3

Tobias Reneau of the Ninth Street Nightjars was projected for 19.3 and scored 8.1. The Nightjars lost by 23.7 to the Halyard Bay Harriers, an upset, and dropped to 4-3.

I ask Trevor Ashby of the Ninth Street Nightjars for a word on the receiver. No response came back for this piece, so the box score is the only witness I have.

The other candidate is in Ashgrove. Bo Tremaine of the Ashgrove Anvils was projected 16.2, scored 4.8, and the Anvils lost by 45.2 and are on a three-game skid at 2-5.

Bea Coleridge of the Ashgrove Anvils declined to comment. That is a fact and I print it as one.

## Best Late-Round Pick: I Cannot Give This One Out

I asked for the draft board. It did not come through this week.

So I am not going to pretend I know who went where and how late. I will hand out the closest honest thing instead: the biggest gap between a projection and a Sunday.

Ivan Petrosky of the Sable Ridge Sentinels was projected 14.9 and scored 28.6. That is on an 0-7 team. He was the best player on the field in the closest game of the week and it bought his team nothing.

I ask Ruth Tanaka of the Sable Ridge Sentinels whether that performance changes anything about the back half. She talked to me about the bench instead, and that conversation is in the next section.

Elsewhere on the transaction wire: the Milltown Mudlarks put 34 down on the waiver claim for Rudy Halvorsen on Wednesday morning, then lost by 6.4. Owen Radcliffe of the Milltown Mudlarks did not respond to a request for comment sent Monday. That is half a conversation, and I have had shorter ones that went worse.

The only trade on the books is from Week 6: the Quarry Road Quakers sent Hank Osterberg to the Foundry District Foxes for Juno Castellanos and Vic Trawick. Both teams won in Week 7. Neither general manager went on the record about it, and I did not ask them to defend a deal that has not finished happening yet.

One more from the sideline. In Week 5 I said Sable Ridge does not win a game before Thanksgiving. That one is still open, and they draw the Pike County Pilots in Week 8.

This is the part where I hold the mic and wait.
