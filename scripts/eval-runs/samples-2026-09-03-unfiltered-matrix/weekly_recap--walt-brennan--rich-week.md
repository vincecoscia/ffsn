# The Bench That Cost Sable Ridge Its First Win

## The argument, stated up front

Opinion, mine, and I'll take the weight of it: the Sable Ridge Sentinels did not lose week seven to the Gravel Pit Grinders. They lost it to their own caution. Zero and seven going on eight, and the game they finally deserved to win arrived on schedule and got declined at the door. Everything else in this league right now is a straight line — Gravel Pit is 7-0, Cedar Falls is 5-2 and stacking points, Halyard Bay stole one it wasn't supposed to have — and Curtis and Nina can walk you down the column of numbers at their leisure. I'm interested in the lineup card. A season is a mortgage; you pay it in small decisions, monthly, and the interest is the part nobody photographs.

## Sable Ridge had it, and put it down

The Gravel Pit Grinders won 134.2 to 128.9, a margin of 5.3, which is the kind of number you can hold in one hand. Rex Dolan went for 31.8 for the Grinders and that was the honest part of the afternoon; Dana Whitlock's team has been paying its mortgage on time all year and I said as much back when I credited the Grinders' waiver discipline, so I won't pretend to be surprised.

The other side of it is harder to look at. The Sentinels got 28.6 from Ivan Petrosky, started Cole Vandermeer for 6.2, and left Duke Ellery on the bench — Ellery would have replaced Vandermeer, and that swap is worth 17.9 points. Sable Ridge scored 78.6 sitting down. A bench is a confession, and this one didn't whisper; it said shit out loud, in a room where the door was already open by 5.3.

Ruth Tanaka and I have not agreed on much this season and we are not going to start now, but I'll say the fair thing: the second answer is more honest than most managers manage in October. That's the last time this column says the name. I told you what I thought in week five, I've now told you in week seven, and the record can carry it from here without my help.

Caution is a bill too. It just arrives later, and it arrives by the pound.

## Everything else, as far as it goes

The Cedar Falls Cormorants beat the Ashgrove Anvils 141.6 to 96.4, a 45.2-point verdict, with Cass Lindqvist at 33.4 doing most of the persuading. Marcus Bly's team also spent 12 on Trey Ballinger this week, which is the sort of quiet, unglamorous grocery-shopping that keeps showing up in the points-for column; I said in week four that Cedar Falls would finish top two in points for and I'm not going to be modest about it now that it's holding. The Anvils, meanwhile, started Bo Tremaine for 4.8 against a projection more than three times that. I asked. Bea Coleridge declined to comment, and that is her right, and the column goes on without her.

The Quarry Road Quakers held off the Milltown Mudlarks 118.7 to 112.3. Milltown had the best player on the field — Femi Adeyemi, 26.3 — and lost by 6.4 anyway while carrying 47.2 on the bench, which is what happens when the top of a lineup outruns the bottom of it. Owen Radcliffe's team spent 34 on Rudy Halvorsen on Wednesday morning; I asked about the week. Owen Radcliffe is entitled to silence, and I'm entitled to the column. Nico Barrantes at 19.7 from a tight end is how Priya Nandi's team keeps winning these — not by being better, by being less short in fewer places.

The upset was the Halyard Bay Harriers over the Ninth Street Nightjars, 127.5 to 103.8, and it was not close, which is the insult inside it. Ninth Street was projected 126.2 and scored 103.8; Tobias Reneau gave them 8.1 and the bench gave them 61.5, and Trevor Ashby now gets to spend a week explaining why the strongest part of his roster watched. Gil Provost went 29.9 for the Harriers and Lena Okafor's team didn't need a story beyond that.

I like Lena Okafor's answer more than I like most answers, and I'll argue with it anyway: she cared, everybody cares, the not-caring is a costume you put on after the scoreboard agrees with you. It's a good costume. Wear it.

The Foundry District Foxes handled the Pike County Pilots 121.4 to 99.6 behind Dane Kowalczyk's 27.2. Pike County left 55.3 on the bench and added Nate Brill on Wednesday, which is a plan the same way a shopping list is a dinner. Sam Kestrel's team is 3-4 and in the last playoff seat, which tells you what kind of league this is in the middle.

Six teams make the playoffs and four of them are already sorted. The other two seats are going to be decided by people who forget to check a Sunday injury note.

## Who showed up and who didn't

Cass Lindqvist's 33.4 was the top score of the week and it wasn't a rumor; the Cormorants projected him at 17.6 and he doubled the forecast, which is what forecasts are for — being read by people who never go outside. Rex Dolan at 31.8 for the Grinders, Gil Provost at 29.9 for the Harriers, Ivan Petrosky at 28.6 for the Sentinels and Dane Kowalczyk at 27.2 for the Foxes rounded out the honest work.

The other list is shorter and meaner. Bo Tremaine gave the Anvils 4.8. Cole Vandermeer gave the Sentinels 6.2 in a game decided by 5.3. Tobias Reneau gave the Nightjars 8.1 on a Sunday they were favored to win by a comfortable stretch. Three men, three lineups, three managers who had a chance to do something else on Sunday morning and did this instead.

I've watched managers do dumber things than starting a 6.2 over a 24.1, but not many, and not for free.

A good week is when your best players play. That's it. That's the whole trade.

## On the record

Three managers went on the record this week and each of them said something truer than they probably meant to. Dana Whitlock, asked about 7-0, refused the compliment. Ruth Tanaka, asked about a benched receiver, gave a reason and then gave a number. Lena Okafor, asked about the upset, gave the shortest answer of the three and the most useful one.

What I take from all of it is that this league's managers understand their own weeks better than their lineups suggest. Understanding is not the hard part. Setting the thing at noon on a Sunday when the report says questionable — that's the hard part, and no quote ever got a point back.

Somebody is going to learn something from this week. I've done this long enough not to bet on who.
