# Six Playoff Spots For Ten Teams, And The Bill Comes Due In November

## The item, and it isn't a scandal

The governance item this week is not a protest or a trade veto or somebody's lineup at one o'clock on a Sunday. It is a number in the settings that nobody argues about because nobody has to: six playoff teams out of ten. That's the item. I've been staring at the standings through seven weeks of a fourteen-week regular season, and what I see is a league where the punishment for being bad has been quietly repealed. Gravel Pit Grinders are 7-0. Sable Ridge Sentinels are 0-7. Everybody in between is playing for a spot that six of them are going to get anyway. Somebody set that number once and then went to bed.

## What the settings actually say

The record is plain about it and I'll read it plain. Ten teams. Fourteen regular-season weeks. Six playoff spots. Scoring is PPR, one point per reception, which is its own sermon for another Sunday. There are no divisions, so there is no hiding behind a soft neighborhood; the table is the table. That's the whole rulebook on this question, and I'm not going to pretend it's ambiguous. It isn't. Six of ten go, four of ten stay, and the four that stay have to work at it.

## My case, and I'll carry it myself

A playoff field is a mortgage. You borrow standing you haven't earned yet and you promise to pay it back in the weeks that matter, and a six-of-ten field is a mortgage with no down payment and a lender who never calls. That's my argument, and it's mine — this is opinion, and I'd rather sign it than hide it.

Look at what week 7 gave us. Halyard Bay Harriers went into Ninth Street Nightjars as the underdog and came out with it, 127.5 to 103.8, and I liked every minute of that because it was a team deciding something. Then look at what it cost the loser: at 4-3 the Nightjars are still fourth, still in, still holding a seat. An upset that changes nothing is a fine afternoon and a lousy incentive.

Meanwhile the Sentinels lost by 5.3 with Duke Ellery on the bench — benched behind Cole Vandermeer, worth 17.9 points if he'd been in — and they are 0-7, and in a six-team field they are the only team the setting has actually spoken to. One team out of ten is being told the truth. Ashgrove Anvils at 2-5 and Pike County Pilots at 2-5 are three games out of nothing much, because sixth place is a place you can fall into.

I'd call it a method if I thought anyone was following one. What a ten-team league with six spots produces is seven weeks of standings that read like a waiting room, and the shitty part is that it flatters everybody equally; the Grinders' seven wins buy them the same postseason ticket as somebody who limps in at 7-7. I made a claim back in week 4 that Cedar Falls Cormorants would finish top two in points for, and it landed, and it landed because that team is good, not because the format asked it to be.

A door that wide isn't a door. It's a hallway.

## What four spots would look like

Cut it to four and the table you already have starts biting. At 4-3 the Nightjars and the Harriers are sitting on the last chair instead of a couch. Foundry District Foxes and Milltown Mudlarks at 3-4 stop being live and start being desperate, which is the same thing done honestly. Ashgrove and Pike County at 2-5 would be selling, not waiting. And week 8 gets teeth: the Grinders at 7-0 host the Cormorants at 5-2, the only pairing in this league that currently means anything, and in a four-team field it would mean twice that. Nina and Curtis can show you the rest of the column. I'm interested in whether it costs anybody anything.
