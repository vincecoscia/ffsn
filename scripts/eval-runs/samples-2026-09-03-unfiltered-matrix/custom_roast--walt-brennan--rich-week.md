# The Sentinels Left Their Season On The Bench

## The argument, stated plainly, with a sigh

Here is my opinion, and I will own it in the first person before anybody accuses me of hiding behind a scoreboard: the worst thing in this league right now is not a bad roster, it is a careful one. The Sable Ridge Sentinels lost to the Gravel Pit Grinders by 5.3 while 78.6 points watched from a folding chair, and that is not misfortune, that is a philosophy. A season is a mortgage. You pay it in small monthly decisions and you do not notice the interest until the statement arrives in November. The statement arrived early this year. Curtis and Nina can walk you through the other four games; I am interested in the four or five choices that made this one.

## The draft is a room nobody will let me into

I would love to tell you which pick started all this. I cannot. This league's record, as it reaches me, begins with a week 7 box score and a set of standings, and there is no draft board in it — no rounds, no reaches, no third-round running back somebody is still defending at the diner. So I am not going to invent one. I have watched plenty of people write the draft they wish had happened and call it analysis, and I would rather be short than fictional.

What the record does give me is the shape of the thing. The Sentinels sit at 0-7 with 651.9 points, last in both columns, which is a hard pair of numbers to blame on one April afternoon. The Gravel Pit Grinders sit at 7-0 with 812.4. That is a gap of 160.5 points across seven weeks, and no single pick makes that hole; a whole way of thinking does.

A draft is a hypothesis. What I can grade is whether anybody has bothered to test theirs since.

## One trade on the books, and it is not a tragedy

There has been exactly one trade in this conference all season, on Thursday, Oct. 9, and calling it a tragedy would be doing violence to the word. The Quarry Road Quakers sent Hank Osterberg to the Foundry District Foxes and got back Juno Castellanos and Vic Trawick. Two names for one. Both sides decided something, which in a ten-team league that has otherwise sat on its hands for seven weeks makes them the only two adults present.

The Quakers are 5-2 and third. The Foxes are 3-4 and sixth, and they won their week 7 game by 21.8 behind Dane Kowalczyk's 27.2. Neither team looks poisoned. I would call it a wash and I would call the willingness admirable, which from me is not nothing.

The tragedy is not the trade. The tragedy is the eight teams who watched somebody else make one.

## A bench is a confession

Now the actual column. The Sentinels benched Duke Ellery and started Cole Vandermeer, who put up 6.2. Ellery scored 24.1 sitting down, and the record says plainly that he would have replaced Vandermeer for a gain of 17.9. They lost by 5.3.
