# The Case For Duke Ellery, Who Scored 24.1 From A Chair

## First, The Honest Column Against Him

Class. Before anyone frames this man, look at the row that argues against him.

Duke Ellery was projected for 11.7 in Week 7. Projected. That is a suggestion, and the suggestion was that he is the fourth-most interesting receiver in a sentence about a last-place roster. He is on the Sable Ridge Sentinels, who are 0-7 with 651.9 points scored, last in the league in both columns. Nobody's case is easy from the bottom row.

And here is the honest part: one game. One. That is not a sample, it is an anecdote with a decimal point. I have exactly one box score line for Ellery in front of me and no season-long usage, no snap counts, no target share. I don't have that, and I am not going to pretend otherwise.

So grade the case against him: partly supported. Partial credit for the projection, partial credit for the roster he plays on, none at all for what he actually did on Sunday. Circle the difference. We are about to spend the rest of the segment on it.

## Now Show Your Work

Everyone look at the board. Week 7: Sable Ridge lost to the Gravel Pit Grinders 134.2 to 128.9. Margin, 5.3. The results desk downstairs calls that a nail-biter and moves to the next game. I am not moving.

Duke Ellery was benched. He scored 24.1. He would have replaced Cole Vandermeer in the lineup, and the gain from that one swap was 17.9 points. Pop quiz: is 17.9 larger than 5.3? It is. That is the whole case, and it fits on one line.

Want the other rows? Vandermeer started, was projected for 15.8, and scored 6.2. Ellery was projected for 11.7 and scored 24.1 — beat his projection by 12.4 (24.1 minus 11.7). One of those two men was the correct start and it was not the one who played.

Bench points for Sable Ridge in that game: 78.6. The Grinders' bench: 41.3. That is a difference of 37.3 points sitting in a chair, and Ellery's 24.1 is the biggest piece of it that had a starter's job available.

Reggie will tell you the scoreboard is the only truth. The scoreboard says 0-7. It is one column, and it does not explain itself. These four do: 128.9, 5.3, 24.1, 17.9. That is a real column. I checked it twice.

The story says a winless team is a talent problem. The column says this particular loss was a lineup problem, and the lineup problem has a name and 24.1 points next to it. Only one of those two readings has a decimal point.

## What Has To Happen

The case for Ellery is not complicated, which is why I like it. It requires exactly one thing: he goes in the starting lineup.

That is it. That is the path. He does not need a trade, a new offense or a narrative. He needs the slot that Cole Vandermeer had in Week 7 — the slot where the swap was worth 17.9 points.

Second condition, and it is smaller: the number has to survive contact with a second week. One game is one game. He was projected 11.7 and scored 24.1; do it again and I will start treating the projection as the thing that was wrong instead of the day that was weird. Until then I hold it loosely. I am holding it loosely. I am also still right about the 17.9.

Third, the schedule cooperates. Sable Ridge is at the Pike County Pilots in Week 8, projected 112.9 against 108.7 — the only projected favorite the Sentinels have on this week's board, and Pike County comes in 2-5 with 664.1 points, second-lowest in the league. If the case for Ellery is ever going to convert into a win, that is the row it happens in.

For the record, I said in Week 5 that Sable Ridge does not win a game before Thanksgiving. That claim is still open. I would love for Ellery to be the reason it closes badly for me.

## The One Thing That Kills It

Named risk: the injury report. Ellery was listed questionable, and the reason his 24.1 is a bench line and not a starter line is that his manager treated questionable as unstartable.

If that designation follows him — if he keeps arriving at Sunday noon as a coin flip — then the case dies of caution, not of production. A player who is never in the lineup does not have a good week; he has a good afternoon that nobody counted.

No hedge on this one. Twenty-four point one on the bench in a 5.3-point loss is, and I am using the technical term, a fucking problem. Moving on.
