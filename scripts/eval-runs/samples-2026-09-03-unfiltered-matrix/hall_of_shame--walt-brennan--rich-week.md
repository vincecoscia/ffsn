# Hall of Shame, Week 7: The Bench That Cost Five Points

## The standard for induction, such as it is

This is a column, not a recap, so let me put the argument up front and then go slow: the shameful thing in week 7 was not losing, it was the careful decision that lost. I have watched enough of these seasons to know the difference between a team that got beaten and a team that talked itself out of a win on Sunday morning, and week 7 in the Ironclad Fantasy Conference gave me one of each and then a third that never showed up at all.

So the standard here is simple, and I will hold to it: a choice somebody made on purpose, that they could explain, that cost them the game anyway. Bad luck is not shameful. Bad luck is weather. What gets inducted is the umbrella left by the door.

## A bench is a confession

Sable Ridge Sentinels scored 128.9 and lost by 5.3 to Gravel Pit Grinders, and they did it with 78.6 points sitting on the bench in street clothes. That is the sort of pair of numbers I have learned to sit with for a while before writing anything, because writing it fast makes it sound like a joke, and it is not a joke, it is a mortgage payment skipped.

Duke Ellery was benched. He would have replaced Cole Vandermeer, who started and put up 6.2, and the swap was worth 17.9 points. The margin was 5.3. You can do the small subtraction yourself; I have done it three times now and it comes out the same way every time.

Ruth Tanaka is right about the second part and I disagree with her about the first, and I want to be precise about why: a questionable tag at noon is a weather forecast read by someone who never goes outside. It is a hedge against a zero that cost the game anyway. The Sentinels are 0-7 with a seven-game losing streak, and the record only goes back as far as this league's record goes back, so I will not tell you it is the worst bench in its history. I will tell you it is the one I will remember. And this is the last time I write about them this season — I said it, and I meant it.

A bench is a confession. This one didn't whisper.

## Thirty-four dollars for a name I can't find

The only trade on the books is week 6, and it is not shameful: Quarry Road Quakers gave Hank Osterberg to Foundry District Foxes and got back Juno Castellanos and Vic Trawick. Two bodies for one. The Quakers won and the Foxes won, so I will let that one alone; I am not going to induct a deal both sides can still defend.

The waiver wire is where the money went sideways. On Wednesday morning, Oct. 15, at 9:02 a.m., Milltown Mudlarks claimed Rudy Halvorsen for thirty-four dollars and dropped Pete Sandoval to do it. Two minutes later, Cedar Falls Cormorants claimed Trey Ballinger for twelve. One of those teams then scored 141.6 and won by 45.2. The other lost 112.3 to 118.7, by 6.4, and I could not find Rudy Halvorsen anywhere in the part of the box score that mattered.

I asked. Owen Radcliffe is entitled to silence, and I'm entitled to the column. So here is the column: thirty-four dollars is not a number, it is a statement of confidence, and a statement of confidence you make on Wednesday has to survive Sunday. This one did not have to survive much, because nothing was asked of it.

The bill for a bid arrives whether or not the man plays.

## Ninety-six point four, in a league that averages more than that

Ashgrove Anvils scored 96.4 and lost by 45.2 to Cedar Falls Cormorants, and that is the worst number filed this week. They were projected for 108.2, which tells you the shortfall was not the plan; Bo Tremaine started, was projected for 16.2, and delivered 4.8, which tells you where the hole was. The box score does not tell us why, and I am not going to make something up to fill it.

Bea Coleridge declined to comment, and that is her right; the record notes it and I will not read anything into it.

What I will read into is the standings. Three straight losses, 2-5, 688.6 points on the year. That is not a bad week, that is a slow leak in a pipe somebody keeps deciding to look at next month.

A score like that doesn't lose a game. It concedes one.

## The induction

Into the Hall of Shame, week 7: the decision to sit Duke Ellery, worth 17.9 points, in a game lost by 5.3 — the clean, purposeful, defensible-sounding choice that was wrong in exactly the amount the scoreboard needed. Alongside it, the thirty-four dollars Milltown Mudlarks spent on a Wednesday and never cashed, and the 96.4 Ashgrove Anvils filed without ever getting near their own projection. Three installments skipped. Dana Whitlock of Gravel Pit Grinders, who runs the only 7-0 team here, said it plainest when I asked about the streak.
