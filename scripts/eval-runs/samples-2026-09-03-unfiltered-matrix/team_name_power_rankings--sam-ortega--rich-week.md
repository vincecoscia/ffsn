# The Name Rankings: Ten Signs on Ten Doors

## How I'm judging these, once

I am ranking the names. Not the teams. A 0-7 team can own the best sign in the building and a 7-0 team can own a shrug.

Three things, applied the same way to all ten. One: does it sound like a place. Two: can I say it once, fast, into a microphone, and be understood. Three: does it survive being read out after a loss.

Records are context here, not criteria. Nina has the rest of the numbers. I have the doors.

## Ten names, one to ten

**1. Gravel Pit Grinders.** Three hard syllables in a row and none of them are decoration. It sounds like a place with a gate and a schedule. The 7-0 record is coincidence; the name would rank here at 0-7.

**2. Sable Ridge Sentinels.** Yes, 0-7. Yes, second. The name is a ridge line with something standing on it. That is the job.

**3. Quarry Road Quakers.** A road, a hole in the ground, and a soft second word that lands strangely against the first. I like the strangeness. It is the only name here that sounds like an address you could actually drive to.

**4. Ninth Street Nightjars.** A nightjar is a bird almost nobody can pick out of a lineup, which makes the name feel like it knows something. Ninth Street does the work of grounding it.

**5. Halyard Bay Harriers.** Rope, water, a running dog. It moves. It is also the only name in the league I have to slow down for on air, which costs it three spots.

**6. Milltown Mudlarks.** Mudlark is the best single word in the conference. Milltown is the most generic. The two halves are fighting and the fight ends in a draw.

**7. Foundry District Foxes.** Two-thirds of an excellent name. Foundry District is heat and brick and shift whistles, and then a fox trots through it. Swap nothing but the animal and this is top three.

**8. Cedar Falls Cormorants.** Pretty. Genuinely pretty. It also sounds like a regional airline, and this is a league where the second-place team just won by 45.2 points. The name is not carrying that.

**9. Ashgrove Anvils.** An anvil is an object that gets hit. That is a bold thing to put on your own door. Ashgrove is lovely and does nothing to help.

**10. Pike County Pilots.** No county in this league needs pilots. It is clean, it is sayable, it is completely inert. It is a name that will never be quoted back at anybody, which is its own kind of result.

## The bottom, and the fix

Pike County Pilots and Ashgrove Anvils close this out, and both fixes are one word.

Ashgrove Anvils needs a noun that swings instead of one that gets swung at. Everything before the second word is already good.

Pike County Pilots needs a reason. Give me the county doing something. Right now the name is a form filled in correctly.

Cedar Falls Cormorants at eighth is the one that will get me mail. I stand on it. Back in Week 4 I said Cedar Falls finishes the season top two in points for, and that one has held up — the name still sounds like a puddle jumper.

Neither Owen Radcliffe of the Milltown Mudlarks nor Bea Coleridge of the Ashgrove Anvils went on the record about their names this week. That is two doors I knocked on and did not get an answer at, and I ranked the signs anyway.
